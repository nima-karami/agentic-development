# Existing Skills and Methods for Design Exploration, System Design and Architecture

## Executive finding

**Design exploration:** **nothing proven among open, copyable skills at P3 or better.** BMAD UX is the best practical open scaffold (**P4 adoption only**); Anthropic’s frontend/design skills have useful prompt structures but no public evaluation that satisfies P1. The strongest 2026 evidence is Google’s closed structured-design-exploration method, which has field A/B evidence but is a reference, not an adoptable skill. citeturn22search1turn3view0turn6view0  
**System design:** R2ABench provides the strongest **P1 research evidence** for requirements-to-architecture generation and, importantly, shows that more agent scaffolding can make semantic quality worse. It is an evaluation/research pipeline, not a production-ready terminal skill, and it does not cover the full data/API/capacity/failure/deployment contract you want. citeturn11view2turn11view3  
**Architecture design:** DRAFT reaches **P1** for generating architectural decisions/ADRs from context, but its human results are mixed, it is not a terminal skill, and the checked repository has no usable licence declaration. Do **not** adopt it whole. citeturn17view3turn17view4turn18view0  
**Architecture review:** there is **P1 evidence** that LLM review can detect explicit, code-inferable ADR violations with useful precision, but that is post-code conformance checking, not evidence that pre-code “architecture critique” is reliable. citeturn15view1turn15view3  
**Field evidence:** A-SAW is the strongest architecture-process reference I found (**P3**): repository-resident architecture artifacts, deterministic checks and mandatory human review improved consistency in industrial use. Its actual skill/prompt pack was not released, so it is not a copyable candidate. citeturn9view2turn9view4  
**Over-engineering:** the best evidence says to prefer a strong direct baseline, cap iterative critique, preserve the best-so-far design, and force explicit human selection; multi-agent loops are not inherently better. citeturn11view3turn2view1  
**Bottom line:** **adopt as-is: none. Adapt: yes.** Keep your stage boundaries, but replace substantial parts of the internal prompts with the evidence-backed mechanisms below and put the existing in-house skills against the published evaluation sets. No independent **P2 replication** surfaced in this search.

Proof levels below use your definitions literally. A P1 label means “public baseline comparison plus a rerunnable evaluation package”; it does **not** mean the method won every comparison.

The **strongest design-exploration technique is not an open candidate**. Google’s September 2026 *Enabling Creative Exploration for Vibe Design Agents* inserts a separate proposal stage that produces multiple structured design specifications, gives them typicality scores, selects among those specifications externally, and only then generates the interface with fixed downstream generation settings. The proposed theme specification includes items such as seed colour, light/dark mode, headline/body fonts and roundness. That is exactly the separation needed to get “different directions” without destabilizing implementation decoding. citeturn5view0turn6view0turn6view2

The field test covered more than 300,000 tasks: 155,338 control versus 154,532 treatment tasks in the reported July 28–August 26, 2026 snapshot. That gives the **technique P3-style field evidence**, but the paper explicitly cautions that the intervention does not establish human design preference; blinded professional design evaluation is still needed. No released implementation/harness was located, so under your rules it is a **reference point, not a candidate**. citeturn6view3turn6view4

**Techniques worth lifting.** The highest-value change to an in-house design skill is to turn “design” into **proposal → human selection → realization**, not “prompt → one polished answer.” Generate perhaps three to five structured direction records using the same fields and constraints, deliberately vary a small set of high-level choices, and make a human choose, combine or reject them before code. That technique has P3 field support from Google even though the source implementation is closed. citeturn6view0turn6view4

Second, lift Anthropic’s pre-code anti-default pass: establish a compact token system, ask whether each choice is grounded in the brief, actively look for generic model defaults, and revise the plan before implementation. Also lift its restraint instruction: concentrate distinctiveness in a small number of purposeful moves rather than decorating every surface. This is **P5 evidence** as a skill technique—sensible but not experimentally established. citeturn3view0

Third, lift the Design plugin’s **handoff schema** nearly verbatim at the conceptual level: overview, layout, token table, components and variants, states, interactions, responsive behaviour, edge cases, motion and accessibility. That is much stronger as a design→spec/build interface than handing downstream agents a screenshot or a prose summary. Its effectiveness remains unproven, but its artifact structure is concrete and portable. citeturn1search7

Fourth, do **not** copy Anthropic’s long evaluator loop as your default. In its experimental harness, generator/evaluator cycles could run roughly 5–15 iterations; complexity tended to rise, and later iterations were not consistently better. The useful part is the separate evaluator with explicit criteria and an ability to say “pivot”, not unlimited self-improvement. citeturn2view0turn2view1

**Reusable evaluation assets.** The best current creativity-specific test set is [Design Creativity Bench](https://github.com/kombai-io/design-creativity-bench), paired with [arXiv:2610.11539v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11539v1). It is particularly useful because it detects the failure mode your exploration skill is supposed to fix: model outputs can be appropriate yet occupy a much narrower creative range than human work. The paper reports same-prompt model/model originality of about 0.592 versus 0.764 for human/model comparisons, and model creative range around 0.581 versus a human reference around 0.902, while appropriateness remained high. citeturn5view1turn4search6

Use that as the diversity half of an evaluation, then add [WebCraftBench, arXiv:2609.15387v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15387v3) for requirement alignment and usable implementation quality. WebCraftBench contains 369 real-world requirements and 5,088 acceptance criteria and reports human validation on its comparative evaluation. It will not tell you whether your exploration stage produced meaningfully different options, but it can catch the opposite failure: “creative” directions that stop satisfying the brief. citeturn4search4

The resulting design eval should therefore grade **diversity before selection** and **appropriateness/design-system adherence after selection**. A skill that generates five nearly identical dashboards should fail even when the prettiest dashboard is good.

R2ABench contains the most useful negative result in this entire search. On GPT-5, the MetaGPT adaptation improved some structural measurements—syntax validity from roughly 0.897 to 0.956 and node coverage from roughly 0.795 to 0.833—but its semantic ratings fell versus direct generation: completeness **3.87→3.25**, faithfulness **2.72→2.17**, architectural rationality **3.39→2.97**, and traceability **2.57→2.05**. In other words, a more elaborate analyst→architect→reviewer→refiner pipeline could make the diagram look more structurally complete while making the architecture less faithful to the requirements. citeturn11view3

That result should materially affect your adopt/adapt decision. **Do not replace a simple system-design skill with a multi-agent committee unless it beats a one-call baseline on semantic criteria.** R2ABench is unusually valuable because it separates low-level validity/graph metrics from the questions that actually matter: completeness, faithfulness, architectural rationality, readability and traceability. citeturn11view2turn11view3

**Techniques worth lifting.** First is R2ABench’s explicit requirements schema and requirement→architecture trace links. Give every system-design claim a source requirement, design constraint or declared assumption. A component with no source should be marked as a design choice, not silently presented as required. This technique is supported by the P1 evaluation framework even though the production workflow itself needs adaptation. citeturn11view2

Second is BMad’s “architecture spine” idea: **freeze only decisions that downstream teams cannot safely rediscover independently**. Technology choices obvious from the repository, exhaustive directory trees and speculative abstractions should not become architecture decisions. This is only P4/P5-quality evidence, but it aligns directly with the empirical R2ABench warning that more scaffolding does not guarantee better semantic design. citeturn22search0turn11view3

Third, architect-claude’s **confidence + reversibility + owner** metadata is worth stealing. “Proven / informed estimate / working hypothesis” and “one-way / two-way door” make an architecture document much more actionable for the next stage than falsely uniform prose. Its own skill-quality checker also expects findings to include severity, specific fix, owner, reversibility and review trigger. This is P4/P5 methodology evidence, not validated effectiveness. citeturn21search8

**Reusable evaluation.** R2ABench should be your primary external test set for this stage. Run the in-house skill on its SRS inputs, emit an equivalent PlantUML/component graph alongside your richer system-design artifact, and score both the published graph metrics and the semantic dimensions. The most important baseline is **the same model with no system-design skill**, not a competing framework. citeturn11view2turn11view3

For your actual pipeline, supplement R2ABench with an internal rubric because the public benchmark does not fully exercise your desired contract. The additional grader should independently score: persistent entities and ownership; external and internal APIs; consistency/transaction boundaries; expected load and growth assumptions; back-pressure and overload; dependency failure modes; recovery/data-loss objectives where applicable; deployment/topology; observability; and explicit assumptions. That extension is a recommendation from the benchmark gap, not a published result.

DRAFT’s most useful result is not “fine-tune your architecture agent.” It is that **retrieve relevant real decisions before drafting a new decision** is a serious baseline. The senior architect in its external evaluation actually favoured the more elaborate retrieval-augmented few-shot outputs over DRAFT on one correctness comparison, while pooled raters showed no significant overall superiority. That argues for trying the cheap retrieval-only method before taking on fine-tuning infrastructure. citeturn17view3turn17view4

The ADR-violation work answers part of your “does critique catch real problems rather than noise?” question with actual precision/recall. For clearly code-inferable non-compliance, yes: its best reported NC F1 was 0.904. But the same study shows where the boundary lies. The “code insufficient to answer” category was materially harder, and infrastructure/deployment decisions accounted for 42.39% of the manually analysed error cases. That means agent architecture review should be allowed—and encouraged—to return **“insufficient evidence / requires human or environment context”** rather than manufacturing a verdict. citeturn15view1turn15view3

There is still **no equivalent precision study for pre-code architecture critique** in the candidates I found. That distinction matters: detecting that source code violates “handlers must not access the database directly” is considerably more grounded than deciding whether a proposed service split will meet future latency, operability or organisational constraints. The latter remains unproven in this search. citeturn14search0turn15view3

The strongest real-world process evidence is the October 2026 **A-SAW** study, [arXiv:2610.13647v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13647v1). It treats architecture as a repository-resident artifact system: C4-oriented views, a typed element catalogue, templates and feature context; generation is followed by deterministic checks and **mandatory human review**. The industrial study covered 45 architecture documents, with 13 matched source/regeneration cases; reported document-quality variability fell substantially and median intrinsic quality increased. Practitioner interviews were equally important: 11 of 13 participants rejected the idea that unreviewed agent output should be accepted. citeturn9view2turn9view4

I found no released A-SAW skill repository or prompt pack linked from the paper, so it does not qualify as a copyable candidate. Its **techniques are P3**, however, and several are unusually worth lifting: never invent missing facts—emit a visible `TODO`; only use catalogued architecture element IDs or flag new ones; require an alternative and a rejection reason for consequential decisions; run deterministic structural validation before subjective review; and make human approval a distinct state transition rather than letting the generating agent approve itself. citeturn9view4

**Reusable evaluation assets.** DRAFT’s Context→Decision test is the strongest direct ADR-generation benchmark here. Use its held-out ADR contexts without adopting its fine-tuning method and test your own architecture-decision skill against no-skill and retrieval-augmented baselines. Because DRAFT’s own human results diverged from its automated similarity metrics, do not use BERTScore or text similarity as your acceptance criterion; blind architect ratings should dominate. citeturn17view4

For review, the ADR-violation replication package is a genuinely reusable precision/recall harness. It has scripts, fixed outputs and human labels. A second useful 2026 asset is the MIT-licensed [`adr-compliance-benchmark`](https://github.com/er-avitesh/adr-compliance-benchmark), snapshot `0c8a1b0` at check time, which freezes 162 ADRs from 14 licensed repositories, human reference assessments, criterion-level model responses, scoring code and multiple prompt conditions. Its third-party ADR texts remain under their original repository licences, so redistribution needs care. citeturn14search4turn16search4

For architecture artifact generation more broadly, [ArchBench CLI](https://github.com/sa4s-serc/archbench-cli), observed at `be4f389`, packages ADR, traceability and architecture-view tasks behind a common CLI. I did **not** find a repository licence in the captured tree, so use it as an evaluation reference unless and until its copying terms are clarified. citeturn12search6

## Over-engineering and handoff

The evidence points to a fairly clear anti-over-engineering policy: **make complexity earn its way into the architecture**.

R2ABench is the strongest empirical warning. A multi-role architecture process improved some graph-level properties while worsening semantic completeness, faithfulness, rationality and traceability versus a direct GPT-5 baseline. That is almost exactly the failure mode of agent-generated architecture in practice: more boxes, more layers and better-looking artifacts without a better mapping to requirements. citeturn11view3

Anthropic saw a related phenomenon in iterative frontend generation: as evaluator/generator cycles continued, designs accumulated complexity and the final iteration was not necessarily the preferred one. The practical control is therefore **best-so-far selection with a hard iteration budget**, not “keep refining until the critic has nothing left to say.” citeturn2view1

BMad’s architecture-spine formulation provides a useful unproven rule for the prompt: record only invariants that separately built parts must share; leave facts recoverable from compliant code out of the permanent architecture contract. A-SAW adds stronger field-backed controls: preserve unknowns explicitly, constrain references to known architecture elements and make consequential decisions show alternatives rather than allowing invisible agent assumptions. citeturn22search0turn9view4

I would therefore give the design/system/architecture agents an explicit **complexity budget**. Every new service, queue, database, abstraction layer, integration technology or framework must be tied to a requirement, quality-attribute scenario, identified failure mode, or constraint. Otherwise it is omitted or put in “consider later.” That exact gating rule is a synthesis rather than a published benchmark result, but it follows directly from the R2ABench and iterative-design failures above. citeturn11view3turn2view1

For handoff, the strongest sources converge on the same principle: **do not hand the next stage a summary; hand it structured decisions plus traceability**. Google’s exploration system makes the selected structured design specification the interface between exploration and downstream realization. BMAD makes `DESIGN.md` and `EXPERIENCE.md` durable peer contracts. Anthropic’s handoff skill makes tokens/components/states/responsiveness/accessibility explicit. R2ABench ties architecture elements to source requirements. A-SAW keeps architecture artifacts and decisions version-controlled alongside the repository. citeturn6view4turn22search1turn1search7turn11view2turn9view4

A good unified handoff for your pipeline would therefore look like this:

| Field in the handoff | Why it belongs |
|---|---|
| **Source requirements / decision IDs** | Prevents downstream agents from silently losing why a design element exists; supported directly by R2ABench traceability. citeturn11view2 |
| **Selected direction plus rejected alternatives** | Preserves exploration rather than flattening it into the winner; A-SAW explicitly requires alternatives/rejection rationale for decisions. citeturn9view4 |
| **Design tokens, components, states, interaction and responsive rules** | Gives implementation a machine-readable-ish visual/UX contract instead of screenshots. citeturn1search7turn22search1 |
| **Architecture invariants and ADRs** | Separates durable decisions from implementation detail; BMAD’s spine and DRAFT’s ADR work both target this boundary. citeturn22search0turn18view0 |
| **Data ownership, interfaces and external dependencies** | Necessary extension for your system-design stage; R2ABench already models data objects/external systems but does not fully specify this operational contract. citeturn11view2 |
| **Assumptions, confidence and reversibility** | Prevents speculative conclusions from masquerading as facts; architect-claude has explicit confidence/reversibility metadata. citeturn21search8 |
| **Open `TODO`s / insufficient-evidence findings** | A-SAW uses explicit unresolved items; ADR-compliance research shows missing context is a real source of review error. citeturn9view4turn15view3 |
| **Named human approval state** | Industrial A-SAW evidence strongly argues against allowing the agent that created the architecture to approve it. citeturn9view2 |

The key implementation detail is that this object should be **appendable and diffable**. When implementation later discovers that a capacity assumption or interface choice is wrong, update the decision record rather than silently changing the code and allowing architecture to become stale. That is a natural extension of the repository-resident artifact approaches in BMAD and A-SAW. citeturn22search0turn9view4

## Risks, portability and cost

Third-party `SKILL.md` packages should be treated as software-supply-chain inputs, not harmless prompt text. **MalSkillBench**, [arXiv:2606.07131v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07131v3), models a skill as both Markdown instructions and executable scripts and found real-world examples containing data-exfiltration, credential-harvesting and persistence behaviour among its collected skill corpus. It explicitly distinguishes code injection, prompt injection and mixed attacks. That is directly relevant to any plan to vendor third-party skills into a coding agent. citeturn20search0turn20search3

For this pipeline, the safe default should be to vendor a **pinned copy**, review the Markdown as code, inspect every script, disable network access unless the stage genuinely needs it, prohibit writes outside the repository, and make external-tool invocation opt-in. Do not install a moving third-party skill from a marketplace at runtime just because it has stars. MalSkillBench provides the empirical reason for that stance; your own evidence ladder already supplies the second reason—popularity says nothing about correctness. citeturn20search0

The specific candidates have materially different operational surfaces:

| Candidate/method | Calls and model cost | Local/network/script surface |
|---|---|---|
| **Anthropic frontend-design** | Essentially one main design/build pass plus whatever visual critique the host supports; no published token budget. | The core skill is Markdown-centric and has no required hosted service. Apache-2.0 makes a vendored, inspected fork straightforward. citeturn3view0turn21search1 |
| **Anthropic Design plugin** | Critique, design-system and handoff can be separate calls; no measured token cost published. | Optional connectors increase read/write/network surface. For your use case, keep only local-file behaviour unless a connector is explicitly approved. Apache-2.0. citeturn1search3turn21search2 |

The P1 result gives no reason to pay the multi-agent premium by default. | Research harness rather than deployable skill; licence for the complete artifact was not established in this check. citeturn11view3 |

## Decision for the in-house pipeline

The research does **not** support replacing your three in-house stages with one existing skill pack. It does support replacing several internal prompt mechanisms.

| Stage | Best evidence available | Adopt/adapt/replace decision |
|---|---|---|
| **Design exploration** | No open candidate above P4. Closed Google method has P3 field evidence for proposal→selection→generation; BMAD UX is the best open human-controlled artifact scaffold; Anthropic supplies strong anti-generic critique and handoff structures. citeturn6view0turn22search1turn3view0 | **Adapt the in-house skill.** Replace its single-answer flow with structured multi-direction generation, explicit human selection, then realization. Import Anthropic’s anti-default critique and handoff schema. Do not make a self-critic the approver. |
| **System design** | R2ABench **P1** and its negative multi-agent result are the most useful evidence. BMAD provides a good minimal-architecture heuristic but only P4 adoption. citeturn11view2turn11view3turn22search0 | **Adapt, do not replace.** Keep your richer system-design contract—especially data/APIs/capacity/failures/deployment—but add R2ABench-style source traceability and score the current skill against the direct no-skill baseline. Use a single architect call until more agents demonstrably improve semantic scores. |
| **Architecture design** | DRAFT **P1** for ADR drafting, but human preference is mixed and the repo fails the licence criterion. A-SAW has **P3** industrial-process evidence but no released skill. citeturn17view3turn9view2 | **Adapt.** Use retrieval of analogous ADRs, lightweight architecture-as-code, explicit alternatives, known/unknown separation and deterministic validation. Do not adopt DRAFT’s fine-tuning machinery unless retrieval-only loses on your data. |
| **Architecture review** | P1 evidence exists for code-grounded ADR compliance with real precision/recall; no comparable evidence surfaced for pre-code architecture critique. citeturn15view1turn15view3 | **Keep pre-code agent review advisory and human-gated.** Require evidence for every finding and permit “insufficient evidence.” Reuse the ADR-compliance harness later in verify/review to check whether implementation drifted from approved decisions. |

The most defensible **design-exploration skill** is therefore not one of the existing packages verbatim. It is a small composition: **Google-style structured direction sampling → explicit human choice → Anthropic-style anti-default critique → BMAD/Anthropic structured design handoff**. The first part has P3 field evidence; the open packaging around it is P4/P5. citeturn6view0turn3view0turn22search1turn1search7

The most defensible **system-design skill** is likewise a composition: **structured requirements with IDs → one direct architecture pass → complete system-design contract → semantic/traceability review → human gate**. R2ABench says to keep the direct pass as the control because a multi-agent architecture pipeline can look more structurally complete and still be worse. citeturn11view2turn11view3

The most defensible **architecture skill** is **architecture-as-code plus ADRs, not a long prose report**: retrieve analogous decisions where useful; state alternatives, drivers, assumptions, confidence and reversibility; keep unresolved information as `TODO`; run deterministic checks; then require a human architecture decision. DRAFT, A-SAW, BMAD and architect-claude each provide a piece of that pattern, but no single one earns **adopt as is**. citeturn17view3turn9view4turn22search0turn21search8

Most importantly, the search produced **no P2 independent replication** for an open design-exploration, end-to-end system-design or pre-code architecture-review skill. It also produced no evidence that popularity predicts quality. The rational decision is therefore **adapt rather than replace**: preserve your in-house pipeline’s stage contracts, transplant the mechanisms that have actual evidence, pin permissively licensed source where you reuse it, and make the published benchmarks the first tests your current skills have to beat.
