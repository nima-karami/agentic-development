# Evidence behind guided-learning

Only findings that both research syntheses in
`research/202610/accelerated-human-cognition-A.md` and `-B.md` state independently
are listed here. Grades are the reports' own.

| Rule | Finding | Key sources | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, 5 | Retrieval practice beats rereading for retention, even though rereading feels better at the time. | Roediger & Karpicke (2006) | Replicated |
| 1, 6 | Guidance that helps novices becomes redundant or harmful for experts (expertise reversal). | Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler & Sweller (2003) | Replicated |
| 3 | Worked examples speed novice learning by removing the search for a procedure. | Sweller; worked-example effect | Replicated |
| 5 | Learners who explain examples to themselves learn more (self-explanation). | Chi et al. (1989) | Replicated |
| 5 | People believe they understand mechanisms until asked to explain them step by step. | Rozenblit & Keil (2002), named by B only | Replicated |
| 7 (close) | Spaced review beats massed review; the best gap grows with how long it must last. | Cepeda et al. (2006, 2008) | Replicated |
| Gotchas | Analogies transfer when relational structure matches; comparing two cases beats one. | Gentner, Loewenstein & Thompson (2003) | Replicated (lab) |
| Gotchas | Animation rarely beats an equivalent static graphic. | Tversky, Morrison & Bétrancourt (2002); Mayer | Replicated |
| Codebase | Developers spend a large share of maintenance time navigating code; comprehension follows the task. | An observational maintenance study (report A, about 35% of time on navigation); Xia et al. (2018) (report B, about 58% on comprehension) | Field studies |

## Not used

- **Learning styles.** Matching format to a "visual" or "verbal" learner has no
  support (Pashler et al., 2008). Adapt to prior knowledge instead.
- **Specific AI-and-learning trial figures** cited by report B. They were written
  without web access and have not been verified. The direction (unguarded AI help
  can lower later unaided performance) is consistent with the retrieval findings
  above, so no rule depends on the exact numbers.
